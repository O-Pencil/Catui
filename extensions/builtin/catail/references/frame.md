# Frame the Research Question

Use this playbook to turn a topic, observation, or product idea into an answerable and falsifiable research question.

## Workflow

1. Freeze the observation before interpretation: source, unit, population/system, time, missingness, preprocessing, and whether it was expected or noticed after inspection.
2. State one primary research question. Define constructs, context, target quantity or comparison, scope, and intended use.
3. List candidate hypotheses separately from facts. For each, add at least one rival explanation and one discriminating prediction.
4. Define what observation would weaken or falsify each candidate. Include negative controls where meaningful.
5. Record feasibility constraints, affected stakeholders, and required human or institutional gates.
6. Update `RESEARCH.md`; create claim rows only for actual candidate claims, with status `candidate` and no invented evidence IDs.

Prefer causal language only when the design, intervention/allocation, and estimand support it. Otherwise use descriptive, associational, or predictive language.

## Quality checks

- The question is narrow enough to answer with available units, data, and time.
- Hypotheses make different observable predictions rather than restating the same preference.
- Success and stopping criteria are not defined solely by statistical significance.
- The user can explain what result would change their mind.

Finish with the primary question, the most important unresolved assumption, and the next evidence-reducing action.
